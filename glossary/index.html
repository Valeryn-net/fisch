Fischwiki /Words from the water

The glossary.

From adipose fins to swim bladders.
A few useful words for a closer look.

A

Adipose fin
A small, rayless fin between the dorsal fin and the tail, typical of salmon and trout, among others.
Ampullae of Lorenzini
Electroreceptors in sharks and rays that detect the faint electric fields produced by prey.
Anadromous
Growing up at sea and migrating into fresh water to spawn, as the Atlantic salmon does.

B

Barbel
A whisker-like sensory organ near the mouth, carrying taste and touch receptors. Found on catfish, carp and cod.
Brackish water
A mix of fresh and salt water, as in estuaries or much of the Baltic Sea.

C

Catadromous
Growing up in fresh water and migrating to sea to spawn, as the European eel does.
Chondrichthyes
Cartilaginous fish: sharks, rays and chimaeras.
Clavus
The rudder-like flap that replaces the tail in ocean sunfish.

F

Filter feeder
An animal that strains food, usually plankton, out of the water, like the whale shark.

I

Illicium
The “fishing rod” of anglerfish: a modified first dorsal spine with a lure at the tip.
Invasive species
A species introduced by people to a new region, where it spreads and harms native ecosystems.

L

Lateral line
A sensory system along the flanks that registers water movement and pressure changes.
Leptocephalus
The transparent, leaf-shaped larva of eels and their relatives.

P

Pharyngeal teeth
Teeth on modified gill arches in the throat, used by carp and their relatives to grind food.

R

Regional endothermy
The ability to keep parts of the body warmer than the surrounding water, as in tuna and mackerel sharks.

S

Sarcopterygii
Lobe-finned fish, including coelacanths and lungfish, and the group from which land vertebrates arose.
Scute
A bony external plate, such as the rows along the body of a sturgeon.
Smolt
A young salmon or trout that has turned silver and is physiologically ready to move to the sea.
Spawn
To release eggs and sperm; also the eggs themselves.
Swim bladder
A gas-filled organ that many bony fish use to control buoyancy. Sharks do not have one.
Reading the Red List

Extinction risk,
explained.

The IUCN Red List assesses species globally. The categories VU, EN and CR are collectively called “threatened”.

LC
Least Concern
NT
Near Threatened
VU
Vulnerable
EN
Endangered
CR
Critically Endangered
EW
Extinct in the Wild
EX
Extinct
DD
Data Deficient
NE
Not Evaluated

A global category does not describe every local population. “Least Concern” does not mean that a species faces no pressures.
